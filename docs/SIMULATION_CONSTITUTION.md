# Simulation Constitution

1. No fixed historical technology ladder. Store capabilities, principles, structures, materials and evidence.
2. Terrain is law. Deep water, cliffs and lethal climates remain barriers until an adequate physical capability exists.
3. Material ground truth is hidden from civilizations; knowledge is local, uncertain and revisable.
4. Failed experiments create evidence and may seed later designs.
5. Designs have lineage: mutation, recombination, copying, capture and cultural drift.
6. AI may propose; deterministic simulation decides whether something works.
7. Civilizations may regress, migrate, fragment or become extinct.
8. Monsters are ecological/combat actors, not cosmetic event damage.
9. Scale changes representation, not causality: detailed agents near focus, cohorts/statistics at distance.
10. Preserve significant causal history; aggregate routine history.
11. Simulation complexity must not force visual clutter.
12. Automated scenarios reject impossible traversal, event leakage, data explosion and universal convergence.
